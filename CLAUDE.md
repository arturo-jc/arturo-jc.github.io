# Circuitboard portfolio

A redesign of the portfolio at `~/Code/personal-website`, which it will replace. That repo is the source for content: `_includes/`, `_data/projects.yml`, `_data/articles.yml`, `assets/`. The blog's posts have moved here, into `_posts/`; a new post is written here.

## The guiding principle

**The circuit creates the space for the content.** A section is not a rectangular card laid over a decorative background. The circuitry itself establishes where a section begins and ends, and the copy sits in the room the circuit leaves for it.

The circuit also has a job: text reads best in a narrow column, and a narrow column floating in empty space looks silly. The circuit balances the column. Too little circuitry and it cannot do that; too much and it competes with the text. A single line guiding the eye is a different idea, and too little.

## The look

The drawing is a flat top-down view of a physical board, with two cues borrowed from schematics:

- From the board: traces with weight that turn at 45°, chip footprints with pad rows, two-pad parts, vias at trace ends, silkscreen designators (`U1`, `R4`), and lamps that are lit and pulse slowly.
- From the schematic: semicircular bridges where one trace crosses another, and wire codes (`A0131`).

The palette is a dark blue ground, light blue traces and an orange accent; the tokens are in `:root` in `styles.css`. Orange is for the one net that leads to the content, and for lit lamps. The mood board is https://ca.pinterest.com/arturojaviercastellanos/circuitboard-portfolio-mood-board/, and its US map is the main reference.

## Narrow screens

Every section has two drawings: a wide one, and a narrow one for phones. Draw both when building a section, without being asked. The narrow one is a first proposal: render it at 390px and show it.

On a phone the copy fills the screen, so there is no floating column for the circuit to balance. What is left of its job is to mark where the section begins and ends and to keep the copy company, and the amount of circuit shrinks to match. The user's preference is for a narrow drawing that is **light, and still runs alongside the copy**: a few traces down one side of it, rather than bands of circuit stacked above and below a full-width column, and rather than a heavier drawing of either kind.

### What every narrow layout keeps

- **The scale.** 32 cells wide, at the same pitch, trace weight and type sizes as the wide layout, so that at 390px one unit is about one pixel and body text about 16.8px. Below 384px the drawing keeps shrinking and the type stops at its design size, 16.5px body text, as it does on a wide screen. It is never the wide drawing shrunk.
- **Room for the copy.** The copy keeps at least 24 of the 32 cells. Circuit beside it goes on one side only, three traces at most.
- **A short top.** 12 to 14 rows above the heading, so the heading and the first lines of copy are on the first screen.
- **The same circuit, pared down.** It is a reduction of the section's wide drawing, not a new one: the main chip, the accent net, and the same designators and codes. Most of the bundles and the outlying chips go.
- **The accent net** still ends on a via beside the heading, level with the middle of its capitals.
- **A beginning and an end.** Something sits above the heading and something past the last line.
- **The rules the drawing follows,** unchanged. A top band, a rail and a tail make three sides of a frame; staggered stops and an open fourth side keep it from reading as one.

### What changes from one section to the next

Sections stack on a phone, so one layout repeated down the page reads as a template. About's narrow layout is an example, not the pattern: J1 and U1 above, three traces down the left of the name and the copy that turn under the copy, the nearest turning down with the other two and then east again, after a short 45° step, into an off-page flag before the link to the blog, and the other two dropping off the board's foot into Projects' U3, and the accent net coming down out of U1 and in from the right along the name's first line. The choices that are free:

- **The rail's side:** left or right.
- **Where the rail starts and ends:** from a connector or from a chip; ending by turning under the copy, by stopping short at staggered heights, or by running into a small part.
- **What sits above the heading:** a connector, a bundle and a chip; a chip alone; a chip with a serpentine.
- **Where the accent net comes in from:** along the heading's row from the right or the left, or down from above.
- **What closes the section:** a staggered tail, lamps, a small chip.

Before drawing a narrow layout, look at the narrow layouts of the sections directly above and below it, and differ from each on at least two of these, starting with the rail's side. Keep the variation modest: enough to break the monotony, within the rules above, not a new idiom per section.

**Decorative parts are optional.** Lamps, a serpentine or a bridge go in where the routing leads to them naturally. Do not bend a layout to fit one in; a narrow layout with no lamps is fine.

These rules come from About, which is the name and two paragraphs. A section whose content is a list, such as articles, may need something they do not cover. Propose it rather than forcing the list into About's shape. Projects and Research are the lists so far. Projects: U3 above the heading, fed through its two top left-hand pads by About's rail, three traces down the right out of U3, and U4 under the last project, which passes a bus on to Research. Its accent net is the rail's nearest trace, which branches in along each project's name, so every item in the list gets its via. Research: that bus turns west into U5 above the heading, three traces leave U5 and run down the left of the papers, and the two outer ones turn under the last paper into two lamps. Its accent net is the rail's nearest trace too, with a short branch in to just before each title, so the vias line up like bullets.

## How design questions get settled

When the user asks to settle a design question, build a **study**: a single page in `studies/` showing several options along one dimension, one after the other, with everything else held fixed so the choice is not biased by a font or a colour that happened to differ. The user picks, and the pick is built into the site.

- Name studies `NN-question.html`. A study is self-contained: one file, with its own copy of whatever it needs from `styles.css` and `js/`, and nothing imported from them.
- Show only the part of the page the question is about. No header or navigation unless that is the question.
- Building the options from one set of switches makes a "mix your own" panel at the bottom nearly free, and lets the user test a combination that is not among the options.

Studies double as a parts bin of working code:

## The code

The site is built by Jekyll, as GitHub Pages builds it (the `github-pages` gem in the `Gemfile`), which serves it for free. Jekyll only assembles the pages: every drawing is fitted in the browser, from the type as it is measured there, so a build never changes the geometry.

The home page has three sections and a footer. About opens the page: the name, which is the page's `h1`, the About copy, and a link to the blog at `/blog`, the end of a net that leaves the board through a schematic off-page flag. Projects follows: its heading and two projects, each a name that links to its repo, a stack and a paragraph. Research is the last: its heading and four papers, newest first, each a title that links to the paper's draft, a PDF in `assets/pdf/` at the path it had on the old site, the journal, which links to the published article, and a summary. Under it, outside `main`, is the footer: a band in `--foot`, darker than the board, edge to edge, with no drawing in it, holding the original portfolio's three icons (email, GitHub, LinkedIn) and the copyright, centred.

The blog is two kinds of page, each with one board, and the board holds only the page's head. The index, at `/blog`, the address it had on the old site (Jekyll writes it as `blog.html`, which GitHub Pages also serves at `/blog`), has the heading "Blog", which the off-page flag `A0140` arrives at, the same net that leaves About beside its link. A post has its way back to the index, a link behind a flag pointing at it in the top left, its title and its date. Both heads are a masthead: a connector on the top edge and a bus across the top into the chip. Everything under the head (the posts with their dates and excerpts, or a post's body and its Disqus comments) is plain text in a column on the boards' grid, outside the board, with no drawing beside it. On every page but the home page, the name in the footer links home.

The 404 page, which GitHub Pages serves for any address it has no page for, is one board: a big "404", the old site's two lines, and a link home behind an off-page flag. Its drawing is a cut trace: the accent net leaves U301 orange, stops on a via short of the heading, and picks up past a gap in blue, because nothing reaches the heading. It is short, so `main` grows to fill the window and the footer sits at its foot.

### Adding a section

1. In `index.html`, add a `<section class="board" id="articles">` holding an `<h2 data-seat="head">` and the content, in one block or several, each with a `data-seat`. Give a block of running text the class `copy`, which styles its paragraphs and links.
2. Create `js/sections/articles.js`. It imports what it needs from `../kit.js` and ends with `export default { wide, narrow }`. `js/sections/projects.js` is the model for a section with a heading and several blocks.
3. In `js/main.js`, add a loader for it to `sections`, under the section's id, and add the id to `boards` in `index.html`'s front matter, so the head preloads it.
4. Give every link to another site `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`, so it opens in a new tab. A link to a page on this site, such as `/blog`, opens in the same tab and has neither; a link to a PDF on this site, such as a paper's draft, opens in a new tab like one to another site. In a post, `_layouts/post.html` adds them to every link that starts with `http`, because kramdown cannot and GitHub Pages runs no plugin that does: write a post's links as plain Markdown, and a link to this site as a path, not a full URL.
5. Add to `styles.css` only what the content needs that `.copy` does not style yet. A new kind of part goes in `js/kit.js`, beside `chip()`, `part()` and `flag()`.

### How a section is drawn

- **Units.** Geometry is in grid cells; one cell is one trace pitch (`P` units). A section's drawing is a `cols` × `rows` grid.
- **A section** is a `.board` element containing its blocks of type, mounted with `mountBoard(board, { wide, narrow })`. A block is anything with a `data-seat`: About's are `name`, `copy` and `blog`, Projects' are `head`, `p1` and `p2`, Research's are `head` and `r1` to `r4`. Without the scripts the blocks are an ordinary column.
- **About's heading is the name,** an `h1.name` beside the copy rather than an `h2` inside it, so that a layout can seat the two apart. Each of its three words is a span. `styles.css` sets it on two lines on a wide screen (Arturo / Javier-Castellanos) and on three on a narrow one, in the same media query that picks the layout.
- **A layout** is `{ cols, seats, build(m) }`. `seats` has one entry per block, keyed by its `data-seat`: the cell where the block's top left sits, its width `w` if it wraps, and its `size` in units if the layout sets it, as About does for the name. A seat can be a function of the blocks seated before it, which is how one block sits under another: `p2: m => ({ x: 54, y: f(m.p1.bot + 7), w: 36 })`. `drawBoard()` seats and measures the blocks in order and passes the measurements to `build(m)`, which returns the circuit and its `rows`; the circuit is `wires` and any of `chips`, `parts`, `labels` and `flags`, each an off-page flag `{ tip, y, code }` drawn by `flag()`, pointing east, or west with `dir: -1`; `go: true` marks the flag in front of a link, which warms when a seated link on its board is hovered. `drawing()` in `js/kit.js` turns it into SVG. `m` has the same keys, in cells from the board's top left: `m.copy.top`, `.bot` and `.h` are a block's box and `.l` its left edge; a block with a heading also has `.cap`, the row of the middle of the capitals of the heading's first line, and `.hx`, the column where that line's last letter ends. The heading is the block itself if it is an `h1`, an `h2` or a link, and otherwise its `h3`.
- **Layouts are built from `m`:** in About, everything beside and below the copy hangs off `m.copy.top` and `m.copy.bot`, and the accent net's row is `m.name.cap`. Build new layouts this way. A chip that the accent net leaves in a straight line is placed from that `cap` too, so it and what hangs off it sit on fractional rows: U1 in About's wide layout, U3 and U4 in Projects'.
- **Projects' and Research's accent nets end beside each item's name or title,** not beside the section's heading: just past it where the net comes from the right, just before it (`x − 1.5`) where it comes from the left. Research's titles wrap, so "just past" means past the first line: `hx` is that line's end, not the right of the heading's box.
- **`bundle(centreline, n)`** returns `n` parallel traces that nest correctly round every corner. Write the centreline with right-angle turns; the 45° cuts are computed. `cut()` ends a trace early. `wire()` is a single trace; `hops` marks where it bridges. A third argument, `{ cornerIndex: units }`, sets the size of a corner's cut and makes a long 45° run; two corners whose cuts add up to the length of the segment between them make one straight diagonal. To split a bus, build two bundles of the full width whose centrelines share their first corners and `slice()` a different half from each. About's wide diagonal is one such half, `slice(3)` of a six-wide bundle.
- **The circuit animates with the scroll,** as set out under "The animation" below.
- **Each layout is fixed** and scales as a whole, its type included, down to the type's design size. Type is set in `--t`, which is `--u` but never below 1px: below about 1350px on a wide screen, and 384px on a narrow one, the drawing keeps shrinking and the type does not, so it wraps into more lines and the layouts, built from it as measured, grow to fit. That keeps the copy at 16.5px or more and lets zoom enlarge it. Set new type in `--t`. The name, the blog's and the 404 page's big headings and a post's title stay on `--u`, because a layout places the drawing against them by their size (the diagonal past the name's end in About's wide layout), and so do a seat's `top`, the footer's icons and anything else that is not type. The narrow one is drawn below 1100px, grows with the screen up to 448px wide, and is centred beyond that, so a tablet gets the phone drawing with margins.
- **The breakpoint is written four times** and all must match: the three `max-width: 1099px` media queries in `styles.css`, the boards' (which also sets the name's line breaks), the blog's `.flow` and the footer's, and `NARROW` in `js/board.js`.
- **The footer is on the boards' grid without being a board.** It has no layout or script; its CSS scales with the same units, 1284 wide and 384 narrow, and centres the icons on cells 6 to 98, Research's two columns, which puts them under the gap between them rather than at the window's middle. `main` has no bottom padding, so Research's drawing ends straight onto the band.
- **The blog's text is on the boards' grid without being on a board.** `.flow .col` sets its own `--u` from its container's width, as a board does, and its type is in `--t`, so it scales with the drawing down to its design size, and sits at cells 30 to 84 of 107, 54 wide, about 725px on a wide screen, the width of the column the user chose as a reference (Michael Huemer's Substack), or 6 to 30 of 32 below the breakpoint. Those are the columns `js/sections/blog.js` and `post.js` seat the heading, the title and the date at: move one and move the other. The head's board ends where the text starts, its `rows` a few under the heading or the date, so a new post changes nothing but the index's length, and a post's drawing depends only on its title.
- **`mountBoard()` redraws** when the board's width changes, when the page crosses the breakpoint, when the fonts arrive and when a seated block's height changes at the same width, because the copy wraps differently at another width or in another face, and is taller under a reader's text-spacing override, a translation or text-only zoom. `build(m)` therefore runs many times: build fresh wires in it, or build them once outside it and never `cut()` them inside. Every board is redrawn in the same turn. A width or height change within one layout redraws only once the sizes have been still for `SETTLE` (150ms), and only the boards whose width or block heights differ from those they were drawn at, which keeps a redraw's own resizing of the blocks from setting off another: until then the drawing and the type scale together as they are, which keeps a drag of the window's edge smooth, where a redraw every frame costs about 50ms a frame on the wide home page. Crossing the breakpoint redraws at once.

### The animation

`js/rails.js` draws three kinds of run, marked in the layouts. Everything else is drawn from the start.

- **The main rail** runs chip to chip down the page: U1 → U3 → U4 → U5 → U6 → U7 → U8 on a wide screen, J1 → U3 → U4 → U5 on a narrow one. `rail(to, wires)` marks the traces into chip `to`, in both boards when they cross a seam. They follow the scroll: each is drawn out of its source so that its tip reaches `to` as `to` comes up to a reading line 70% down the window. Its drawn length is a function of the scroll position alone.
- **A branch** leaves a chip on the main rail and stops short, on vias or at a header. `rail(name, wires, after)` names it for where it goes (`'U4 west'`) and gives the chip it leaves. It grows by itself at a steady speed (`GROW`) once the main rail reaches `after`, and retracts four times faster (`RETRACT`) when the reader scrolls back above it. The branches are the bus to J2, the runs west out of U4 (in both layouts), the serpentine out of U5 and, in the wide layout, the lamps out of U5's right side (`'U5 lamps'`). A run that stops short can also follow the scroll, as U8's tail does: mark it with a name and no `after`.
- **About's other runs grow on load.** In both layouts the bus from U1 to J1 is a branch with `after: 'load'`, drawn out of U1 as the page opens. In the wide layout so is the diagonal into U2, and the lamps' traces are a branch after `'U2'`, so they grow out of U2 once the diagonal reaches it, ending on the blog's flag, which is marked `rail` with them. A branch that reaches a chip connects it as the main rail does. Everything drawn on load, these and U1's net, runs on one clock: each chain (U1 → J1; U1 → U2 → lamps → flag; the net) eases out over `LOAD` seconds, so they all finish together however long each is. A chain given a shorter `dur` starts late and still finishes with the others, unless it also sets `wait`, the seconds after load it starts. A main rail marked `from` a chip holds the sweep back until that chip connects, so on a narrow screen the rail grows down out of J1 only once the bus reaches it; on a wide one the sweep starts at load. The narrow layout uses both so that everything out of U1 lands together: the bus into J1 starts at once and takes 0.35 seconds, the rail then follows the sweep, and the net, much shorter, waits 0.15 seconds and takes one, finishing with the sweep. Change `SWEEP` and change the net's `dur` in `about.js` with it.
- **The blog's heads grow on load too,** on the same clock as About's: the bus out of U101 into J101, or out of U201 into J201, is a branch after `'load'`, a post's way back is the branch `'U201 back'`, ending on its flag, and the net into the heading or the title is the chip's own (`net: 'U101'`, `net: 'U201'`). A flag at the end of a net is marked `net` with it, as a part is.
- **A chip's accent net,** everything marked `net: 'U3'`, grows out of U3 in blue once the main rail reaches it, always in the same time (`NET`, half a second) whatever its length, so that every net lights on the same beat after its chip connects; then it fades to orange. It turns blue and retracts when U3 disconnects. A branch of a net that starts on its trunk, as each title's does in Research's narrow layout, waits for the trunk's tip to reach it. A chip with no run into it, as U1 has none, counts as connected from the start, so About's net draws out of U1 on load.

What sits on a run appears as the tip reaches it, so nothing floats ahead of its trace: a via, and a label marked `rail` or `net` with the run's name, when the nearest trace end is reached; a part marked the same way, with its designator, when the tip passes it. Mark every part and label on an animated run, or it is drawn from the start.

On load the reading line sweeps down from the top of the page to its place in `SWEEP` seconds, easing out, so the main rail grows out of U1 instead of appearing half drawn; everything else follows the line, so the sweep needs nothing from them. It covers a fraction of the way to the line's place, so it takes the same time however far down the page opens.

**The type fades in,** on the home page only, once and never back out. The name does not fade; About's copy and link, marked `data-fade="load"`, fade in on load, the link a little after the copy; every other block marked `data-fade` fades in once its top is 85% of the way down the window. `index.html` sets `fades: true` in its front matter, and `_includes/scripts.html` then sets the class `fades` on `<html>` before the first paint, and the CSS hides the blocks only under it and without reduced motion, so the page is readable without the scripts. Fade opacity only: a transform would move the boxes `measure()` reads.

**No type shows before its board is drawn.** Until the modules have run, a board's blocks sit in the plain column a page without the scripts shows, and the first paint comes before that, so without this the name would flash at the top centre, small, before jumping to its seat. So `_includes/scripts.html`, in every page's head, sets the class `js` before the first paint, and `styles.css` hides a board's blocks under it until the board has the class `drawn`. It hides the blog's `.flow` under its head's board the same way: the posts and an article are not on the board, but until the drawing arrives the board is only as tall as its hidden heading, and the text under it would show near the top and jump down 150 to 180px. If the modules never run, the include's timer takes `js` and `fades` away after three seconds so the type shows in that column; `main.js` cancels the timer. The include also preloads the modules the page runs, so that the browser fetches them in one round instead of finding each through its importer: every module in `js/` but the sections, listed by Jekyll, and the sections named in the page's `boards`. A board missing from `boards` is still drawn, but its section is fetched only once `main.js` has run, a round trip later.

A trace is drawn from its first point, so write a run's centreline from its source. A branch's and a net's drawn length is kept by name, so a redraw needs nothing from them. With reduced motion everything is drawn from the start.

### Rules the drawing follows

- **Every wire ends on something:** a pad, a via or another trace. Nothing runs off the canvas or stops bare.
- **Do not close a frame round the copy.** Traces that wrap the text must not read as a border, which would be the card this design exists to avoid. Break the perimeter: a step in a bundle, traces that stop short at staggered lengths, a chip in the run. With right-angle turns a wrapping bundle reads as concentric rectangles; the 45° turns are part of what prevents that.
- **Bridges need room.** Bridge only where the crossed traces are at least two cells apart. At trace weight, bridges over a tight bundle merge into a wavy lump. A bridge is 7 units in radius, so a trace that bridges also needs more than half a cell clear on the side it rises toward (north over a horizontal run, east over a vertical one).

### Footguns

- **About's wide layout does not know how wide the name is or where U1 ends.** The diagonal leaves U1's three lowest left pads about 6 cells past the end of the name's second line and passes about 6 from the copy's top left corner, and the copy's top is a fixed row, about 3 below U1's pads. The rail on the copy's right is at a fixed column, `Xr`, about 4 cells past its longest line, with its outer trace in line with J1's last pin, and takes U1's three lowest right-hand pads, so J1's bus is five wide. Change the name's size or position and check all three; `X`, `yc`, `Yd` and `shoulder` in `about.js` move the diagonal; `shoulder` plus the lower corner's cut must equal `(Yd - yc) × P`.
- **About's narrow rail crosses into Projects too,** but only two of its traces: they drop at columns 13 and 14 (a three-wide bundle centred on 14 in `about.js`, a two-wide one centred on 13.5 in `projects.js`) and turn east into U3's two top left-hand pads. Move U3 or the drop columns in one file and move them in the other. The rail's third trace, the one nearest the copy, turns down with them and east again `step` rows lower into the blog's flag; its two cuts add up to the step, and the first is the bundle's own, so the step stays one straight 45° run nested with the other two. The rail's row under the copy is found from the link's `cap`, `step` rows above it, so move the link and the rail moves with it.
- **About's wide link to the blog hangs off the lamps' return,** which turns down at column 30 and east along the word's `cap` into the flag; the word is seated at column 40, just under the copy's last line, and About's `rows` grows to clear it. Keep the return's run east to the flag's tail at least 4 cells, and the word clear of the copy at column 54.
- **About's wide rail crosses into Projects.** It runs down the copy's right and off the foot of About's drawing; Projects' layout picks it up at row 0 in the same three columns and takes it into U3's three top right-hand pads. The two boards abut and share the 107-column grid, so the halves meet only while both layouts use the same column (`Xr` in `about.js`, 94 in `projects.js`) and width: change one, change the other. U3 sits at the same column as U1, under it. The bus from U3 to J2 drops under the rail with its outer trace in line with the rail's (centre 93, five wide, against the rail's 94, three wide), so it moves with the rail too.
- **Projects' bus crosses into Research, in both layouts.** Wide: it leaves U4's three lowest right-hand pads, drops off Projects' foot at columns 48 to 50 (`bus` in `projects.js`, 49 in `research.js`), and Research carries it across its top into U5. Narrow: it leaves U4's bottom pads at columns 25 to 27 (centre 26 in both files) and Research turns it west into U5's right-hand pads. Move U4 or the column in one file and move it in the other.
- **Research's wide right column is dropped from the first title's `cap`** (`r2` at `r1.cap + 32`), which is what leaves U5 and its lamps room in the top right-hand corner and keeps U7 and U8 apart in the gap. The two lower papers sit 8 rows under the ones above them in the same column, so the chips in the gap are spaced by the papers' heights; shorten a summary a lot and check U7 and U8 still clear each other.
- **Projects' wide diagonal is sized from the two names' rows.** U3 ends 8 rows below the first name's `cap` and the bus enters U4 3 rows above the second's, so the corner's cut is `(a2 − a1 − 11 − drop) × P`, where `drop` is the straight run under U3. The second project's seat, 7 rows under the first, is what leaves U4 room under the first project's last line. Change either chip's height or pads and change the 11 with it.
- **Do not simplify how `drawBoard()` swaps the drawing.** It leaves the old `svg` in until the new one is built, because without it the page is shorter, a scrollbar can go, and the type is then measured at a width it is not shown at. Across the breakpoint it does the opposite and removes the old one first, because the other layout's drawing stretched to the new width can make the page tall enough to gain a scrollbar and fall back across the breakpoint.
- **`js/rails.js` puts the animation back on a new drawing in a microtask, not a frame later.** A new drawing is fully drawn until `collect()` and `update()` have run, and a redraw can happen in the browser's resize step, just before it paints: deferred to the next animation frame, every trace, via and code shows for one frame. The microtask runs once however many boards were redrawn together.
- **`measure()` reads the board's box beside each block's, not once before seating.** Seating the blocks changes the page's height; scrolled to the foot, the browser then pulls the scroll back and every on-screen box moves. A board box read earlier puts the blocks' rows off, which showed up as Research's wide drawing nearly twice as tall after a trip to the narrow layout and back.
- **Line the drawing up with the type by measuring, not by arithmetic.** The accent trace ends level with the middle of the heading's capitals, and working that out from font size and line height puts it in the wrong place. `measure()` does the measuring and returns it as `m.cap`: a zero-size inline-block at the start of the heading's first line gives the baseline, and canvas `measureText('H').actualBoundingBoxAscent` gives the cap height. It measures the cap height at 1000px and scales it down, because at text size the canvas reports whole pixels. A measurement taken before the fonts arrive is of the fallback face.
- **A bundle needs length between two corners.** Each corner's 45° cut is up to `6 + 0.586 × (n − 1) / 2 × P` units deep, and two corners share the segment between them. Give a 5-wide bundle at least 4 cells between turns and an 8-wide one at least 6. Shorter than that, the cuts are scaled down and the diagonals stop being parallel.
- **An even-width bundle's centreline sits on half cells** (`10.5`), so that its traces land on whole cells and meet the pads. The first trace returned is the leftmost in the direction of travel.
- **Parts and via holes are painted in the ground colour.** A resistor or lamp hides the trace under it with a `--bg` rectangle, and a via's hole is a `--bg` disc. Both break if the ground stops being one flat colour, and a part must sit on a straight run with nothing else passing under it.
- **Near the copy's edge, align exactly or miss by a clear cell.** On the narrow grid most things sit close to the copy's left or right edge, and a 2px miss reads as a mistake. A chip's body is drawn 3 units inside its box and a connector's outline exactly on it, so a chip whose body should line up with copy at cell 2 has `x0: 1.75`.
- **Designators are drawn from the chips and parts, not written as labels.** A chip's is its `id`, placed by `ref` (`top`, the default, `top-end`, `left`, `below` or `corner`; a connector's `right`, the default, or `pins-left`), and a part's is its `ref`, which takes the part's `net` or `rail` with it. `nudge: [dx, dy]` moves either. Move a chip and its designator follows.
- **Labels collide silently.** A label character is about half a cell wide, and nothing checks a label against traces, parts or the copy. After adding or moving one, zoom in on the render.
- **`--u` and `--t` are defined on the seated blocks, `[data-seat]`, not on `.board`.** It is built from a container-query unit, which resolves against an ancestor container, and `.board` is the container.
- **The lamp halo's blur is in CSS pixels,** so unlike everything else it does not scale with the drawing.

### Looking at the page

Render the page and look at it; a description of the code is not evidence. The user notices misalignment and uneven spacing, so measure before reporting.

- **The page has to be served.** The scripts are ES modules, which do not load from `file://`: opened that way, the page shows the copy and no drawing. The Chrome tools cannot open `file://` URLs either. A study can be served with caching off by Python, and the server stopped afterwards:
  `python3 -c "import http.server as h; H = h.SimpleHTTPRequestHandler; e = H.end_headers; H.end_headers = lambda s: (s.send_header('Cache-Control', 'no-store'), e(s))[1]; h.ThreadingHTTPServer(('127.0.0.1', 8741), H).serve_forever()"` and `fuser -k 8741/tcp`. Stop it by port, not with `pkill -f` and a pattern, which also matches the shell running it when the pattern appears anywhere in that shell's command. Plain `python3 -m http.server` sends no cache headers, and Chrome then loads the page with some modules new and some stale, which shows up as a section with no drawing.
- **A section is taller than the screenshot.** Set `document.body.style.zoom` to fit it for an overview, then use the zoom action on bridges, labels and trace ends, where the problems are. Take measurements at zoom 1.
- **Look at the narrow layout in iframes,** several widths side by side. On the served page, hide `main` and inject same-origin iframes of the page at 320, 390, 430 and 768px wide; media queries inside an iframe follow the iframe's width, and its `contentDocument` can be measured from outside. Give each iframe `box-sizing: content-box` and more height than the page, or its border and a scrollbar come out of the width. Check at each width that there is no horizontal scroll, that the accent via is level with the heading's cap middle, and that the gap under the copy's last line is the same. Then change one iframe's width across 1100px in both directions and check the drawing swaps.
- **Headless Chrome's window is never narrower than 500px.** A `--window-size` under that lays the page out at 500px and squeezes the screenshot to the width asked for afterwards, with the type in the wrong seats. To screenshot a phone width headlessly, serve a page of iframes at those widths from another port and screenshot that.
- **The pages are served by Jekyll,** with the system Ruby. The gems are in `vendor/bundle`, set by the ignored `.bundle/config`; in a fresh clone, run `bundle config set --local path vendor/bundle` and `bundle install` first. Serve at http://127.0.0.1:4000 with `bundle exec jekyll serve`, which sends no-cache headers and rebuilds when a file changes, and stop it with `fuser -k 4000/tcp`. Jekyll does not reload `_config.yml` while serving: restart it after changing that. It also serves `404.html` for a missing address only if the file existed when it started; otherwise open `/404.html` directly.
  Opened without Jekyll, `index.html` shows its front matter as text and has no head or footer. The studies are not part of the site (`_config.yml` excludes them) and still open from the plain server above, or straight from disk.
- **Mood board images:** navigating a tab straight to an `i.pinimg.com` image hangs the screenshot tool, and `/originals/` URLs fail. Stay on the board page, read the pin image URLs from the DOM, swap `/236x/` for `/736x/`, and show the image in an element injected into that page.

## Committing

Commit without being asked after a significant batch of work: a section built, a study finished, a pick built into the site.

Do not commit small adjustments made so the user can see how something looks, such as a font size, a colour or a spacing nudge. When the wording is exploratory ("let's try…", "what if…"), or the request is a vague tweak of the kind someone makes before they have made up their mind, leave it uncommitted. It goes into the next significant commit, or gets committed when the user says they are happy with it. When unsure which case applies, leave it uncommitted and say so.

The repo has no remote and no gitleaks hook: do not push, and say the commit was not scanned.
