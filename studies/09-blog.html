Study 09

The blog: an index and an article

The two blog pages, built from your four posts. Both put the text in one column, 36 cells wide, with a rail of three traces down its left. On the index, the trace nearest the text is orange and taps in to just before each post's title, as Research's does on a phone, and the other two turn under the last post into two lamps. The heading carries an off-page flag coded A0140, the same net that leaves About through the flag beside its "Blog". On the article, an orange net from U201 runs to the title, the rail taps in to each section's heading in blue (only links get orange), the other traces stop short under the last paragraph, and a flag at the top points back to the index.

What changes is what keeps the rail from being one long straight line: A leaves it straight; B steps it two cells sideways at each post and each section, on a 45° run, alternating in and out; C keeps it straight and puts a resistor or a capacitor on it between one tap and the next. On a phone the rail sits in the 6-cell margin the copy leaves, and there is no room for B's steps, so B's phone layout is A's.

Each option shows the index, then the DevOps post (seven sections, the longest title) cut off after its third section. The panel at the bottom draws any post in full, with its foot, and also switches the taps between a 45° knee and a square T.
